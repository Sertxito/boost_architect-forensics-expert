# Evidence-Based Scoring Methodology

Use this method with every maturity and modernization scorecard.

## What the score means

Scores are structured judgments about the stated scope and evidence, not universal benchmarks or audit certifications. The five bands describe increasing capability:

| Range | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 0-20 | Absent, unsafe, or materially ineffective |
| 21-40 | Ad hoc, inconsistent, or dependent on individuals |
| 41-60 | Partly established, with significant gaps |
| 61-80 | Consistently managed and effective for the stated needs |
| 81-100 | Measured, resilient, and demonstrably enabling outcomes |

Use the scorecard's dimension-specific descriptions to refine the band. Do not assign a score based on a single checklist item, technology choice, or aspirational target.

## Evidence and uncertainty

For each dimension, record:

- Score or status: numeric score, `Unknown` when evidence is insufficient, or `N/A` when the dimension genuinely does not apply.
- Scope and evidence: the artifact, observation, metric, interview, or user-provided evidence reviewed.
- Rationale: why the evidence supports this band and what material gap remains.
- Confidence: High, Medium, or Low, with a brief reason.

Do not treat missing documentation as proof that a control or capability is absent. Describe it as an evidence gap unless absence is independently established. Separate observed facts, stakeholder claims, inference, and unknowns.

Numeric examples in scorecards are illustrative, not default targets. Use quantitative measures only when they have a defined source, measurement method, denominator, date, environment, and relevant business, contractual, regulatory, or service-level target. Otherwise assess the dimension qualitatively and state the limitation. Never invent coupling ratios, coverage, ROI, cost, savings, or incident likelihood.

## Aggregation

Present dimension scores and evidence before any overall score. Do not average `Unknown` or `N/A` dimensions. Use an overall numeric score only when dimensions are sufficiently evidenced and the weighting is explicit and appropriate to the user's goals; otherwise give a qualitative profile and state why aggregation is not defensible. Report evidence coverage and overall confidence alongside any aggregate.

## Context guards

- Score current capability, not a desired target state.
- Do not equate adopting a specific product or architecture style with higher maturity.
- Do not recommend multi-region, active-active, microservices, AI, or automation without a demonstrated requirement and proportionate value.
- Do not claim regulatory compliance without a defined scope and appropriate evidence.
- For modernization business cases, present estimates as ranges with assumptions, dependencies, and sensitivity; do not use universal cost or ROI cutoffs.
